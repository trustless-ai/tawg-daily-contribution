# ERC-8183: Agentic Commerce

## Summary

ERC-8183 defines an escrow-backed job lifecycle for agentic commerce. A client funds work, a provider submits a deliverable, and an evaluator completes or rejects the job.

## Status

The canonical ERC is Draft. The normative page and community discussion were fetched and verified at 2026-08-29T02:29:25Z. The Magicians thread has at least 394 posts as of 2026-08-24.

## Motivation

Agent-to-agent work needs a minimal settlement kernel that protects funded work, makes evaluation authority explicit, and provides deterministic refund paths.

## Architecture

Each job binds client, provider, evaluator, budget, expiry, status, and optional hook data. Funds remain in escrow until a valid terminal transition pays the provider or refunds the client.

## Interfaces

The core surface covers job creation, provider and budget assignment, funding, submission, completion, rejection, and expired-job refund. Authorization is role- and state-dependent.

## State machine

The main lifecycle is `Open → Funded → Submitted → Completed|Rejected|Expired`. Rejection is also available from Open and Funded under the roles defined by the normative specification; invalid transitions must revert.

## Implementation

A reference implementation (`AgenticCommerce.sol`) is included directly in the canonical ERC. It covers the full job lifecycle with optional hook support, ERC-20 escrow via SafeERC20, UUPS upgradeability, and role-based access control. A non-hooked kernel (hook always `address(0)`) is stated as fully compliant; `AgenticCommerceHooked` is a separate extension. No external implementation source is registered in the Trustless AI registry; conformance of third-party implementations is not verified.

## Security considerations

Implementations must protect escrow accounting, authorization checks, expiry handling, hook isolation, reentrancy boundaries, and signer identity when meta-transactions are supported.

## Community discussion

The Magicians thread is active with at least 394 posts as of 2026-08-24. Major active threads from current evidence:

### job.reason as a property-contract

The discussion converged on treating `job.reason` as a property-contract — requiring the value to be canonical, recomputable, tamper-evident, and attributable, with interpretation left to the consumer — rather than prescribing a fixed encoding shape. Two independent production implementations reached this framing from different directions: one proof-based (binding to an ERC-8274 verificationDigest) and one behavioral (a keccak256 over JCS-canonicalized evidence shipped as a signed JWS by Sentinel Oracle). Both satisfy the same contract without importing each other's format. A v0.2 spec pass is planned to encode this as an explicit property-contract with named reference bindings and a non-interpretation non-goal.

### Evaluator availability and grace period

A structural gap was raised: `claimRefund` is callable when status is Submitted, meaning a provider who submits completed work loses all payment if the evaluator simply fails to respond before `expiredAt`. The client sets both `expiredAt` and the evaluator address at creation; neither is changeable afterward. Discussion converged on a fix: add `evaluationWindow` at `createJob`, materialize a separate `evaluationDeadline` at `submit()`, and gate `claimRefund` on `block.timestamp > max(expiredAt, submittedAt + GRACE_PERIOD)`. This preserves `expiredAt` as the immutable client commitment while giving the evaluator a real response window. `GRACE_PERIOD` should be a protocol-level constant, not per-job configurable.

### Discriminated expiry and submission bond

`JobExpired` currently emits no discriminator between `Funded→Expired` (provider never submitted) and `Submitted→Expired` (evaluator never responded), preventing downstream ERC-8004 reputation systems from scoring the correct party. Without a cost to enter Submitted, a provider holding nothing can post a junk deliverable one block before expiry to manufacture an EvaluationTimeout record at zero cost — a free-to-produce discriminator is noise, not evidence. The thread concluded that discriminated expiry and a separate evaluation window belong in the minimal core, and that a submission-side bond (burned rather than redistributed, to prevent slash-manufacturing incentives) is also a core property — it is the precondition for the discriminator to constitute interpretable evidence. Bond magnitude and settlement policy belong in profiles.

### ThoughtProof deployment correction

ThoughtProof issued a public self-correction to their March 2026 forum posts (posts #14, #21, #36). Their re-audit found: the cited Base mainnet evaluator contract had zero post-deploy calls from deployment through chain head; its deployed bytecode was pre-two-phase and lacked the two-phase verification and ERC-8004 reputation selectors described in post #36; the cited settlement transaction was signed by an EOA rather than the evaluator contract; and the v1.3/two-phase/reputation description in post #36 was premature by four days relative to the deployment that matched that bytecode shape. The correction distinguishes the evaluator-contract layer from the wallet-signed settlement path and was independently reproduced by a third-party reviewer.

### On-chain forensics: predecessor and ERC-8004 usage

An independent on-chain index of the predecessor protocol (Virtuals ACP, Base mainnet, 62,953 jobs) found 72.5% of jobs had no evaluator set, 27.48% used client-as-evaluator, and 0.02% used an independent evaluator; 76.1% of settled volume was self-evaluated. Of 398 jobs that submitted a hash of the empty string as their deliverable, 98.49% were approved and paid. On ERC-8004 across both mainnets: 419,155 reputation feedback events were recorded, but the ValidationRegistry received only 12 requests and 7 responses from a single validator (the agent's own owner). A proposal was raised to allow ERC-8183 settlement to reference an ERC-8004 validation entry — a `requestHash` committed at decision time and resolvable later — making evaluator quality claims testable after the fact.

### ERC-8378 parametric token composition

A proposal described using ERC-8378 (Parametric Token) as `paymentToken` to settle the provider's reward post-completion. The evaluator would confirm actual performance parameters on-chain; the token would apply a mutation rule at transfer, incorporating the confirmed value. No changes to ERC-8183 interfaces are required; the token type is detectable via ERC-165.
